The press dug up an old remark from Dario Amodei. The Anthropic CEO, back in his OpenAI days, said in an internal discussion that when AI displaces human work at scale and society looks about to boil over, one option might be to settle people down with immersive games. The press found a word for it: pacification. The backlash was considerable — bread and circuses, treating people as batteries. I'll hold off on the outrage.

My own read first: on balance, I am optimistic.

Human history, plainly stated, is the history of ransoming muscle back from the business of eating. Hunter-gatherers spent every waking hour finding food. Agriculture tied people to land and had them watching the sky all year. Industry tied them to machines and let the clock count the hours for them. Each time, some people got their hands free — to paint, to write poems, to wonder why the stars do not move. AI's step is larger than any of the previous ones: people will no longer have to work in order to eat, and goods will become cheap to the point of not counting.

The life on the other side sounds like paradise: a living share from birth, like retiring early. You could read, stare into space, go somewhere far away and stay half a year.

But I don't expect the road there to be gentle. Previous transitions were never carried by goodwill. Enclosure, unemployment, hunger — decades of grinding before a new set of rules emerged. How long this one hurts, and how badly, will not be decided by technology. It will be decided by institutions, and by whether the people at the top have thought ahead. The pain will not be confined to a minority. The pie will be enormous, but who gets the pie has never been a technical question.

Even if the pie is divided evenly, one thing does not divide: how a person regards himself. My generation was taught exactly one thing from childhood — dignity is bought with work. A craft buys a bowl of rice; the bowl buys self-respect.

Back to Dario. What survives of his remark is a single sentence, and I think it has a point. He simply left out the most important layer: when work disappears, what breaks is not only the wage but a road walked for thousands of years — effort, hardship, then success.

The word achievement sounds abstract; it is not. It is the receipt for "my life was not wasted." What people fear was never idleness — idleness can be very comfortable. What people fear is having amounted to nothing: a life spent, and nothing left behind.

Someone will say there is warmth without a job: your family needs you, your friends can't do without you. True. But being needed and having done something are two different things. Your family indulges you — burn a pot of food and they will still call it good. Achievement requires a world that does not indulge you: rules, difficulty, a ranking, and losing that is actually losing.

Games happen to be shaped exactly like that. Objectives, feedback, ranks, teammates, a raid at eight tonight, and that one boss you genuinely cannot get past. You lose in there, and losing hurts; winning back is what winning means. The thing that most resembles effort–hardship–success right now is a game.

Follow that line and Meta's spending on the metaverse makes sense: the demand is probably real and strong. They were simply early — heavy headsets, thin content, and walking in felt like visiting an empty mall on opening day. Right direction, wrong hour; still a failure.

Which is exactly why the word pacification stings. It does not deny your situation; it moves you aside so you are not in the way. The error is contempt, not direction — games cannot save a society's legitimacy, and the Colosseum never saved Rome; but the Romans did need the games. My point is only that this is not as light a matter as it sounds: when work is genuinely gone, whether we can give people a place to live a life in earnest is not a technical question on the order of pacification. It is a necessity.

As for what that place should look like, I can't say either. I only hope it is more honest than the games we have: a place where losing is survivable, and winning is real.
